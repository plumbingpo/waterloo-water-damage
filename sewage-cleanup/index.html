A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. When it happens in Waterloo, Waterloo Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

Here is what the work covers: standing sewage is pumped out, contaminated water is extracted from the structure, porous materials the sewage touched (drywall, insulation, carpet and padding) are torn out and disposed of properly, hard surfaces are disinfected thoroughly, antimicrobials are applied, and the structure is dried so mold does not follow. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects
  • Stormwater from a sump or downspout should never drain into the sewer, since the extra volume invites a backup
  • Clear tree roots near sewer lines, which can infiltrate and block the pipes
  • Address slow drains promptly, before they become complete blockages
  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog

Signs It Is Time for Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Toilet water with waste in it soaking into carpet or running across the floor
  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one
  • Sluggish drainage at all fixtures, a sign the main line is blocking up
  • Family members falling ill, especially with gastrointestinal trouble, for no obvious reason
  • A rotten egg or sewer smell from the drains or the lowest floor of the home
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors
  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue

Why Hire a Professional for Sewage Cleanup?

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.
