Bathroom Water Damage: What Waterloo Homeowners Should Know

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. Once caulk splits, grout thins out or the toilet seal gives up, each shower and flush sends a bit of water through. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. Our Waterloo team finds where the water is getting in, measures how far it has gone, and dries or removes what is wet.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. Subfloor under a toilet can be soft all the way through while the tile above still looks fine. With thermal imaging and moisture meters, we map what is wet before any demolition, and we open just the sections that will not dry in place.

What It Looks Like

  • Vinyl flooring that curls at the edges or has discolored near the toilet or tub
  • Caulk along the tub or shower that is cracked, peeling, or black with mold
  • Inside the vanity, a base shelf that is stained, soft or out of shape
  • A brown ring or sagging spot on the ceiling directly below the bathroom
  • Baseboard puffing up or paint coming off near the shower wall
  • Dark grout that never seems to dry out between showers
Our Process

How We Handle Bathroom Water Damage

1

Pinpoint the Leak

We test the tub, shower, toilet and supply lines one at a time, and use moisture meters and thermal imaging to see where water has traveled under tile and inside walls.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

We take out rotted subfloor, soft wall board and tile that has come loose. If the toilet was the source, we handle that section as contaminated, and porous materials we cannot clean go.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

Using dehumidifiers and air movers, we dry the wall framing, the subfloor and the ceiling cavity underneath. Nothing is sealed back up until we have treated with antimicrobial and the readings show dry.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

We replace subfloor and backer board, and can coordinate tile, a new toilet seal, fresh caulk and ceiling repair so the leak path is closed, not covered over.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Slow Vanity Drips

Supply valves and drain fittings under the sink can drip for months behind stored bottles and towels. First the bottom of the cabinet swells, and then the wall behind and the subfloor get wet.

Damage Downstairs

Before dripping, water runs along joists and pipes, which can put the downstairs stain several feet away from the real leak. Light fixtures and wiring can be in its path.

Leaking Shower Pan

A cracked pan or liner lets water out under the shower floor every time it is used. The surface looks perfect while the framing below stays soaked.

Mold Behind Tile

Neither grout nor tile is a waterproof barrier without help. If the board behind them stays damp, mold grows on it, out of reach of any cleaning.

If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

The first few days

Water Gets Past

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

First few weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Mold gets going in the wall cavity and on the floor's underside.

One to three months

Signs Start to Appear

The caulk line goes black, the grout gets darker, and tiles work loose. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months and beyond

The Structure Suffers

Subfloor around the toilet or tub turns soft and joists may begin to rot. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.
