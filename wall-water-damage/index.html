Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Waterloo

Think of drywall as a wick. When water reaches the bottom of a wall, the gypsum and paper draw it upward, often a foot or more above the level the water ever reached on the floor. The painted face can look and feel normal while the back side and the insulation behind it are soaked. Looks cannot be trusted here, so every wall gets tested with a meter.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. When the wall is soft, crumbling, moldy or was soaked by dirty water, we make a flood cut, a straight line above the damage, and remove everything below it.

Warning Signs

  • Fasteners and joint lines becoming visible on a once smooth wall
  • Bubbling, wrinkled or peeling paint and wallpaper low on the wall
  • Soft or crumbly spots you notice when pushing lightly on the wall
  • One wall noticeably cooler under your hand than the rest
  • Dark spots or fuzzy growth at corners and behind furniture
  • Staining in a horizontal band just above the baseboard
How We Fix It

Our Wall and Drywall Water Damage Process

1

Find Every Wet Area

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Where the drywall stays, the trim is pulled and airflow is sent inside the wall. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on, usually 3 to 5 days, until the studs and the drywall left in place test dry. The rebuild follows with fresh insulation, drywall installation and finishing, trim and a coat of paint.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Hidden Behind Cabinets

Drywall hidden in back of cabinetry and built-in units tends to be missed and dries very slowly. We take readings through toe kicks and from the opposite side to find it.

Soaked Batts in the Wall

Fiberglass batts soak up water and hold it against the studs. No amount of airflow on the painted side will dry a cavity packed with wet batts.

Wet Outlets and Wiring

Outlets and wiring are mounted near the floor, exactly where the water gathers. Do not use outlets on a wet wall until the circuit has been checked.

Moisture in Exterior Walls

Water in a perimeter wall may reach the sheathing on the far side of the cavity. That layer dries slowly and is checked before the wall is closed.

Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Wicking Begins

Water at the base of the wall soaks into the drywall edge and starts to climb. Both the baseboard and the bottom plate start absorbing water.

Hours 24 to 48

Swelling and Blisters

You may see blistered paint and swollen trim as the damp area moves up the wall. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Day 3 to 7

Panels Lose Strength

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. Within the wall the insulation sags, a smell develops and mold moves over the back of the panel.

After a week

Framing Is Affected

By this point the studs and plates have been damp long enough for mold and early rot. Much more wall now has to be removed and rebuilt.
