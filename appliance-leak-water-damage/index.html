Dealing With Appliance Leak Water Damage in Waterloo

Nearly every appliance leak we handle comes from one of three sources. A washing machine supply hose can split and run at full pressure until someone shuts it off. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. The ice maker line on a refrigerator is a thin tube, and it can drip for months behind the unit. Cleanup is different for each of them.

For homeowners in Waterloo, we begin by sliding the machine out to see how far the water traveled. For a kitchen, the usual places are below the flooring and under the cabinets. Laundry room water goes into the wall at the back of the machine and the neighboring rooms, and with an upstairs laundry, the ceiling underneath. We extract what is standing, measure what has soaked in, and set drying equipment sized to the wet area.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the ApplianceIf the outlet and the floor next to it are both dry, end the cycle and take the plug out of the wall. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Close the Supply ValveWashers have hot and cold valves on the wall behind them. Dishwashers shut off under the sink. The small valve for an ice maker line sits behind the refrigerator or underneath.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterUse towels or a mop on whatever water is within reach, which keeps it from spreading beneath flooring and cabinets. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Leave the Door ShutIf the washer or dishwasher has not drained, leave it shut. The entire load of water may spill across the floor if the door is opened mid-cycle.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartPhotograph the failed fitting, valve or hose, and hold on to the part itself. Both the appliance maker and your insurer may want to look at it.

Coverage

Does Insurance Cover It?

A hose that bursts or a machine that overflows without warning is the kind of sudden and accidental event homeowners policies often cover. What is typically paid is the water damage, while the failed part and the appliance are left out. Slow seepage from a dishwasher or ice maker line is a harder claim, because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Some insurers also ask about the age and upkeep of the hose. Hold on to the part that failed, photograph everything ahead of cleanup, and review what your policy says.

Common Causes

Why This Happens

  • A dishwasher that was installed out of level or with a supply connection left loose.
  • A failed water inlet valve that keeps filling the washer or dishwasher after it should stop.
  • Plastic or copper ice maker lines that kink, crack or loosen at the fitting.
  • Dishwasher parts that wear out, including the door gasket, the drain hose and the seal on the pump.
  • An overflow caused by an overloaded drum or by using too much detergent.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Adjoining Rooms

Water may pour from a washer hose for hours on end. Hallways, bedrooms and closets get wet as it travels under the walls, and carpet and pad are soaked a good distance from the laundry.

Baseboards and Wall Bottoms

On a flooded kitchen or laundry floor, the baseboards and the lower edge of the drywall draw water upward. Since it is out of view, the wall behind the machine tends to be wetter than the rest.

Hidden Mold at the Back

The warm, dark space behind a refrigerator or dishwasher rarely gets checked. Mold on the floor and wall can be fed by a slow drip in that spot well before it is noticed.

Floors and What Is Under Them

Water slides beneath laminate, vinyl plank and hardwood. Boards cup or swell, and the subfloor underneath stays wet long after the surface looks dry.
