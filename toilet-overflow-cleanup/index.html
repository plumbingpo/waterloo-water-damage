Toilet Overflow Cleanup in Waterloo: What to Know

An overflowing toilet seems like something towels can handle, except the water keeps moving past the tile. The gap at the drain pipe, the wall and the space under the baseboard all take water in. The contents of that water matter more than anything. Clean water is what comes out of the supply line or the tank. Bowl water is not clean, so we handle it as contaminated from the start.

In Waterloo, we begin each overflow job by determining the category of water, which decides how the rest is done. Clean water means extraction and drying. For gray or black water we wear protective gear, remove soaked porous items such as carpet pad, and disinfect each surface the water reached. After that, moisture meters go on the wall bases, the subfloor and the room below to make sure no wet spot remains.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut Off the SupplyFind the shutoff valve behind the toilet and turn it clockwise as far as it goes. When the valve is frozen, remove the lid of the tank and hold the float up so filling stops.
  2. Do Not Flush AgainHold off on flushing, and keep the washer, showers and sinks off too. With a blocked drain line, whatever goes down a drain may return through the toilet.
  3. Stay Out of Contaminated WaterBowl water, or any water with waste in it, means kids and pets stay out of the room. Should you have to cross it, wear gloves and waterproof footwear.
  4. Check the Room BelowIf the toilet is upstairs, go check the ceiling directly below it. If it is dripping or sagging, switch off power to that room and move belongings out of the way.
  5. Contain Clean WaterUse old towels to keep clean water from reaching carpet or hallways. Leave contaminated water for a crew with proper equipment and disinfectants.
The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Flushed wipes, excess paper or a small toy caught in the trap.
  • A clog in the trap or drain line that sends bowl water over the rim.
  • Water escaping at the base with every flush because the wax ring has worn out.
  • Blockage in the main sewer line, which pushes sewage back out at the lowest toilet.
  • Tree roots or a collapsed section in the sewer line that slows every drain in the house.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Bacteria in Building Materials

Bowl and drain water leaves bacteria in carpet, pad, baseboards and drywall. Porous materials cannot be reliably disinfected and usually have to be removed.

Vanity and Drywall Wicking

The bottom of the drywall and the toe kick of the vanity take in water and pull it upward. The wall can be wet several inches above the floor while the paint still looks fine.

Odor That Returns

Contaminated water that dries inside a wall base or under flooring leaves a smell that returns in humid weather until the source material is taken out.

Soaked Subfloor

Grout lines and the base of the toilet let water pass under vinyl and tile. The plywood underneath swells, delaminates and can loosen the flooring bonded to it.

Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Toilet Overflow Cleanup

When an overflow is accidental and sudden, like a clog nobody expected or a broken fill valve, homeowners policies often cover it. Sewer or drain backup is often excluded without a backup endorsement on the policy, and an overflow from a blocked main line can fall under that exclusion. Damage from a toilet that leaked at the base for months is usually not covered. Read your policy or call your agent to find out which situation applies to you.

What It Costs

What Drives the Price

  • Whether the water was contaminated, because those overflows take more protection, disinfection and labor.
  • If fixtures such as the toilet or the vanity must come out to reach wet material below.
  • The type of bathroom flooring and whether it can be dried or must come up.
  • If the bathroom is on an upper floor, the wet ceiling or walls underneath add to the job.

How We Fix It

Our Toilet Overflow Cleanup Process

1

Identify the Category

First we determine if the source was the bowl and drain or the supply side. Proper containment and protection are put in place before cleanup starts.

2

Extract and Remove

We extract standing water, pull contaminated carpet pad, baseboards or drywall where needed, and lift the toilet if water has gone under the base and flange.

3

Disinfect Surfaces

Floors, wall bases, fixtures and the subfloor are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products. With Category 3 water this step is done before drying equipment is switched on.

4

Dry and Verify

The bathroom, plus any room below that got wet, is dried with dehumidifiers and air movers. We take moisture readings until the subfloor and ceiling are back to normal levels.
