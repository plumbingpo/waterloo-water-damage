Crawl Space Water Removal Help in Waterloo

Water under the house is easy to miss. The usual clues are chilly, clammy floors, an earthy odor rising from the registers, or a pool of water waiting behind the access door. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. Our team pumps the water out, clears what is ruined and dries the framing before it softens.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. Finding the cause matters just as much, since a dripping water line, a failed drain or groundwater from a storm will flood the area again unless it is addressed.

What It Looks Like

  • A look through the hatch shows pooled water or soupy soil
  • Steel posts, hangers for pipes and duct straps showing orange rust
  • Fiberglass insulation sagging or hanging down between the floor joists
  • Fuzzy patches, pale or dark, spreading across the framing or subfloor
  • Ducts and plumbing lines below the house covered in condensation
  • Flooring overhead that seems chilly, clammy, spongy or springy underfoot

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Most crawl spaces carry wiring, junction boxes and HVAC ducts. When duct insulation is saturated it sends stale air around the home, while flooded wiring creates a real risk of shock.

Rot You Cannot See

The first soft wood is usually hidden, up where joists meet the subfloor or back at the sill plate. We probe the wood to find soft spots.

What You Breathe Upstairs

Much of what you breathe on the main level drifts up from beneath the house, carrying any mold spores and musty smells along with it.

Termites and Other Pests

Rodents, carpenter ants and termites all seek out moist lumber and soggy ground. Once the area is dry it becomes far less inviting.

What We Do

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Safety Check and Inspection

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. Next comes charting the extent of the water and metering the joists and subfloor for moisture.

2

Remove Standing Water

Standing water comes out with submersible pumps and hoses, down to the footing trenches and dips where it gathers. Mud and silt left on the liner are cleared at the same time.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Exposed joists and subfloor are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial where growth has started or is likely.

4

Final Drying and Checks

Low profile air movers and a dehumidifier run under the house until the wood reaches a normal moisture reading. We recheck with a meter and give you the readings and a prevention plan.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

Day one

Puddles Form Underneath

Low areas fill first while the ground underneath takes on water. Batts begin drawing moisture upward and the air below the house turns humid quickly.

Hours 24 to 48

Early Mold Growth

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. Wet batts get heavy and begin to pull away from the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Damp air rises into the living space. Floors may start to cup, rooms smell musty and the wood framing keeps absorbing water.

After a week

Rot Sets In

Wood that stays wet begins to decay. Expect softer framing, rusted fasteners, and a wet environment that draws in termites and similar pests.
