What a Professional Brings to Mold Remediation

Mold remediation is not a DIY project. When a colony is disturbed and nothing contains it, millions of spores go into the air, where they may settle in clean rooms and expose your family more than the mold did before. Capturing and containing the spores during removal takes HEPA-filtered scrubbers, protective equipment for the crew and containment kept under negative air pressure, which is how professionals do it.

A professional job goes after the moisture source too, because that is why the mold grew in the first place. Removing visible mold without correcting the underlying moisture problem means the mold will return, often within weeks. Certified remediation technicians have the training and equipment to identify hidden moisture sources, dry affected areas properly and put solutions in place that prevent recurrence.

How to Tell You Need Mold Remediation

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Discovering a plumbing or roof leak that has been dripping for a long time
  • Cleaning that never lasts, with growth returning to the same surface
  • Signs of concealed moisture such as paint that bubbles, wallpaper that peels or materials that warp
  • An earthy, musty smell that will not go away, mostly in humid or closed-off spaces
  • Furniture against outside walls or items in closets developing speckled patches
  • Discolored or darkened spots near HVAC vents, across ceilings or on walls
  • A flood or leak in the recent past that never got a professional dry-out

Maintenance Tips for Mold Remediation

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Give the crawl space proper ventilation, and think about adding a vapor barrier over the soil
  • Regular filter changes and HVAC maintenance help keep mold out of the ducts
  • Dry wet rugs, towels and spills right away instead of letting them sit
  • Address condensation on windows, pipes and cold surfaces promptly
  • Laundry rooms, baths and kitchens all need working exhaust ventilation that is sized for the space
  • Keep indoor humidity below 60% (ideally 30 to 50%) with dehumidifiers and proper ventilation
