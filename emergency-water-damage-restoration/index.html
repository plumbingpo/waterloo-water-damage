What a Professional Brings to Emergency Water Damage Restoration

In an emergency, the hard part is not mopping up what you can see. The difficulty lies in tracking where the remaining water travelled. Baseboards, wall cavities and the space beneath flooring all take on water in minutes. Our team uses moisture meters and thermal imaging to find it, so the wet areas you cannot see are treated the same night instead of being found weeks later as a smell or a stain.

Truck-mounted extractors, commercial-grade air movers and dehumidifiers matched to the room come with the crew, and no household keeps those around. Working around contaminated water and wet electrical wiring is routine for us, as is logging photos and moisture readings starting in the first hour. Those records are useful if you file an insurance claim. The same team that takes the emergency call sees the work through until your home is dry.

Emergency Water Damage Restoration Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Wrap plumbing in cold, unheated parts of the house so a hard freeze cannot crack it.
  • Locate the main shutoff valve now, and show each person at home how to turn it off.
  • Important paperwork and valuables belong above floor level, most of all on the lowest story.
  • Any trip beyond a few days is a reason to turn the main supply off before you go.
  • Swap worn hoses feeding the washer, sinks and toilets rather than waiting for a rupture.
  • Keep the number of an emergency restoration company saved in your phone before you need it.

How to Tell You Need Emergency Water Damage Restoration

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Water from a floor above is streaming down interior walls.
  • Gallons keep spilling from a ruptured water heater tank.
  • Pooled water is creeping over the floor into neighboring rooms or onto the staircase.
  • The water is from a sewer backup or looks dark and smells bad.
  • Nobody was home when it started, so the length of the leak is unknown.
  • Water is coming through a ceiling, light fixture or electrical outlet.
  • Water has reached the electrical panel, appliances or power strips on the floor.
